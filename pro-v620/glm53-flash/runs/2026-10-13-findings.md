# GLM-5.3-Flash: the batch's ingestion notes and brief VRAM rises

Plan committed 2026-10-13 · Run 2026-10-13 · Status: done

## Question

The [batch record](2026-10-12-bench.md) left two findings unexplained:

1. **Ingestion notes.** None of GLM's 12 notes had all three requested sections, against 12
   of 12 for Flash-Next. Which section is missing, and does GLM write all three when it has
   more tokens?
2. **Brief VRAM rises.** 39 times, the card's VRAM counter sat 1.9–7.6 GiB above the
   server's own VRAM for one sample. What holds that memory?

### What the data already collected shows

**Ingestion.**

- Every request, GLM's and Flash-Next's, ended at the 640-token cap (`finish: length`).
  The prompt asks for five summary sentences, eight key facts and three open questions, and
  sets no length; 640 is the request's `max_tokens`.
- Flash-Next's notes reached `## Open questions` before the cap; GLM's did not contain all
  three headings.
- The suite kept the first 300 characters of each note, so which heading is missing is
  not recorded.
- `sections_ok` is an exact, case-sensitive match on `## Summary`, `## Key facts` and
  `## Open questions` in the reply's `content`.

**VRAM rises.**

- **The rises are sums of the server's compute-buffer pieces.** The residency sampler
  lists the server's buffers. Pieces of 1,890, 1,584 and 1,296 MiB are freed and
  created again hundreds of times per batch.
  - The recurring rises match sums of them: 1,906 MiB is 1,890 + 16, 3,490 adds 1,584,
    and 4,786 adds 1,296.
  - At each shutdown llama.cpp logs its Vulkan compute buffer at 4,797–7,063 MiB, against
    6,884–7,154 MiB reserved at load. The largest rises, 6,815 and 7,064 MiB, match those
    sizes.
- **llama.cpp frees the whole compute buffer before allocating a new one.** When a
  graph needs a different layout, `ggml_gallocr_reserve_n_impl` frees the buffer and
  allocates a new one sized for that graph (`ggml/src/ggml-alloc.c`, b11505).
- **Rises come with changing prompt sizes:**
  - none in the concurrency sweep (fixed 512-token prompts), where the 1,890 MiB piece
    was never created again;
  - 13 in the 40-minute input-length sweep;
  - 20 in the 3-hour workloads.
- **Flash-Next shows the same pattern, scaled to its buffers.** Its 2026-10-10 residency
  files, on the same card, have 1–19 rises per run of about 1.1–1.3 GiB. Its compute
  pieces are 600–983 MiB.
- **A scratch trace saw the mechanism, but cannot be cited.** It ran for 30 s on
  Flash-Next while this plan was written, from code that was not yet committed. On each
  request:
  1. The server freed its compute-buffer pieces.
  2. The driver queued a GPU fill of their VRAM from its release hook.
  3. Within 0.2 ms the server allocated the new pieces.
  4. A kernel worker returned the old pieces' VRAM 14–29 ms later.

  This run repeats that trace from pinned code.

**Hypothesis.** Each rise is a compute-buffer swap. The old buffer's VRAM stays counted
while the driver wipes it, and the server has already allocated the new one.

## What answers it

1. **Ingestion.** Replay the batch's four ingestion requests, keeping the full text:
   once at the 640-token cap, then at 2,048 tokens. The requests use the same documents,
   prompt and sampling as the batch.
   - **The 640-token replay must reproduce the batch's notes:** each depth's content hash
     equals the batch's. Its text then shows which of the three headings are missing,
     by the suite's own check.
   - **The 2,048-token replay** shows whether GLM writes all three sections given room.
     It also shows at which output token each heading starts, counted with the server's
     tokenizer.
2. **VRAM rises.** Trace the kernel's VRAM allocations and frees during the replay, with
   the residency sampler running. The hypothesis holds for a rise if both of these hold:
   - the trace shows the server's freed compute-buffer pieces not yet returned at that
     sample;
   - the rise equals those pieces' size within 64 MiB.

   The record reports:
   - each swap's window, from the server's free to the kernel's return;
   - the size freed, and the task that returned it;
   - whether a fill ran on the freed VRAM;
   - whether any new piece failed to get VRAM (`vram_new_ret` not 0) or sat outside VRAM
     (requested above resident).

   A rise that no swap explains is reported as unexplained. The same trace runs on
   Flash-Next after the restore.

## Method

Code at this branch's head when the run starts.

- `push-harness.sh` stages `pro-v620/` on the host.
- The suite runs in CT 200 from the same commit, overlaid on `/opt/bench-runner` as
  `make bench` does. This commit's `benchmark-doc-ingest.py` keeps each note's full
  text; the batch's kept 300 characters.
- The pinned corpus is already cached in CT 200 at
  `/opt/bench-runner/cache/corpus-f7e762c53388`, with the same checksums as the batch.

```bash

  | ssh pve 'cat >/root/glm53-flash/bench-suite-<sha12>.tgz'

  chown -R bench:bench /opt/bench-runner'
```

- **Each ingestion pass** sends the script's short warm-up request, then 8k, 16k, 32k and
  48k in that order, with the prompt cache off, temperature 0 and top-k 1.
  - The change of prompt size between requests is what makes llama.cpp swap its compute
    buffer, so the passes also serve as the trace's workload.
- **The Flash-Next check** is the restore check: it must answer an 8k-token request with
  about 3.2 GiB free and nothing outside VRAM, as after the batch. The warm-up request
  before it gives one swap each way for the trace.
- **Expected duration: about 1 h.**
  - The 640-token pass: about 11 min of prefill, at the batch's rates, and 4 × 80 s of
    decode.
  - The 2,048-token pass: the same prefill and 4 × about 250 s of decode.
  - Load, restore and the check: about 12 min.
- **Analysis:**
  - The trace's `sync` markers align its monotonic clock with the sampler's wall clock.
  - Each `gem_free` by `llama-server` is matched to its `vram_del` by buffer address,
    and to its fill by the same buffer.
  - Each sampler row is checked against the pieces freed and not yet returned at its
    time.
- **Raw data:**
  - on the host, in `/root/glm53-flash/findings/`: the environment, the two traces, the
    residency files and the client's results;
  - on the Mac, the client's results in
    `pro-v620/results/llamacpp-gpu2-glm53f/findings-20261013/` (gitignored).

## Safety

- **Guests:** CT 123 runs throughout, and CT 200 runs for the client and is shut down at
  the end.
  - CT 120 `llamacpp`, CT 121 `hermes`, CT 140 `kb-rag`, CT 201 and VM 300 `docker-host`
    stay shut down, as the batch left them. Starting any of them again waits for the
    owner's go-ahead.
- **Cards, thermal, DIMMs and the job timeout:** as in the batch's Safety section.
  - The thermal watchdog maps `0000:83:00.0` to `llamacpp-glm53f` while `one-card` is in
    place.
  - The deepest request is 48k tokens.
- **Tracing:**
  - The probes fire only on allocations and frees of at least 16 MiB, a few dozen per
    request.
  - The script removes its probes and its tracefs instance when stopped.
  - If it dies without cleaning up, remove the instance by hand:
    `echo 0 >/sys/kernel/tracing/instances/vram-release/events/enable`, then `rmdir` it.
  - The probes are removed with `echo -:vramtrace/<name> >>/sys/kernel/tracing/dynamic_events`.

### Buffer swaps on GLM — trace 06:42–07:19 ET

The trace holds 3,981 events. Its sync markers agree with the wall clock to within 0.3 ms.

- **66 compute-buffer swaps of 4.8–7.1 GiB.** A swap here is a set of frees by
  `llama-server` within 50 ms, together at least 256 MiB. The most common sizes are 4,799,
  6,799 and 6,967 MiB, the compute-buffer sizes llama.cpp logs at shutdown.
- **Each swap runs in the same order:**
  1. The server's thread frees the old pieces (`gem_free`).
  2. The driver's release hook queues a fill of each piece's VRAM (`vram_fill`).
  3. Within a millisecond, the server allocates the new pieces, and each is cleared
     (`vram_clear`).
  4. A kernel worker returns the old pieces' VRAM (`vram_del`).
- **Release timing:**
  - All 318 freed pieces of at least 256 MiB had a fill queued before their VRAM came
    back, and kernel workers returned every one; `llama-server` returned none itself.
  - From free to return took a median of 51 ms per piece (p90 109 ms, max 122 ms).
  - From a swap's first free to its last return took a median of 110 ms (max 218 ms).
- **No VRAM allocation failed:** `vram_new_ret` was 0 throughout.
  - The 354 new buffers of at least 16 MiB asked for VRAM or GTT, and every one was
    created in VRAM.
  - Three asked for GTT only and were created there.
- **The card counter exceeds the server only inside swap windows.**
  - It read exactly 16 MiB above the server's own VRAM in 2,220 of 2,226 samples. The 16
    MiB gap shows at idle too.
  - The six other samples are the six that fell inside a swap window.
  - Five of them meet the batch record's spike rule. Each equals the pieces freed and not
    yet returned, plus that 16 MiB:

    | Time (ET) | Card minus server (MiB) | Freed, not yet returned (MiB) | Free VRAM (MiB) |
    | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
    | 06:45:15 | 3,490 | 3,474 | 7,008 |
    | 06:50:10 | 4,786 | 4,770 | 5,175 |
    | 06:50:39 | 5,293 | 5,277 | 4,793 |
    | 07:07:42 | 3,490 | 3,474 | 6,266 |
    | 07:11:58 | 1,906 | 1,890 | 7,912 |

  - The sixth read 1,312 MiB above, with its neighbour also raised, so the spike rule
    leaves it out.
- **The batch's spikes are the same sizes.** Its recurring 1,906, 3,490 and 4,786 MiB
  rises all appear here. Here 5 of 2,008 samples are spikes (0.25%); in the batch, 39 of

## Conclusion

1. **Ingestion.** The cap explains the batch's 0 of 12: every note lacked
   `## Open questions` because the 640-token cap cut it inside `## Key facts`.
   - Given 2,048 tokens, all four notes have all three sections, with the eight facts
     and three questions the prompt asks for.
   - They stop on their own at 877–1,145 tokens, and `## Open questions` starts at token
     698–980.
   - The 640-token replay reproduced the batch's text at every depth.
2. **VRAM rises.** The hypothesis holds for every rise in this run. All five fall in a
   compute-buffer swap and equal the pieces freed and not yet returned, plus the card's
   constant 16 MiB.
   - **What happens in a swap:**
     - When a graph needs a different layout, llama.cpp frees its compute buffer and
       allocates a new one; on GLM that is 4.8–7.1 GiB.
     - The driver fills the freed VRAM on the GPU, and only then does a kernel worker
       return it. That took a median of 51 ms per piece.
     - For up to 0.2 s the card counts both buffers; the server's own accounting
       already shows only the new one.
   - **Effect here:** no allocation failed, and nothing was placed outside VRAM. During
     a swap, free VRAM fell as low as 4,793 MiB in this run and 1,007 MiB in the batch.
   - **Flash-Next** does the same with 1.2–1.7 GiB swaps that last at most 36 ms.
   - **Not tested:** what happens when free VRAM cannot hold the new buffer while the
     old one waits to be returned. The kernel could wait, evict, or create the new
     buffer in GTT.
